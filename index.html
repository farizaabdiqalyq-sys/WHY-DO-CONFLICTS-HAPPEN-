Term

Key Concept

1

Aggressive behavior is an action intended to cause injury, pain, suffering, damage or destruction. It can be physical, but it can also be verbal. Screaming at someone, insulting them or making comments meant to humiliate them can be aggression. The key idea is intention: harm is deliberate, whether it is caused by words or actions.

2

Researchers have suggested different explanations for aggression. Some theories focus on biology and instinct: they see aggression as a natural impulse. Other theories explain aggression as learned behavior, shaped by what people observe and what happens after they act.

3

Some instinct theories suggest that aggressive impulses can build up and may be directed at other people. One term for redirecting an impulse toward someone or something else is displacement. Another idea is catharsis: an impulse may be expressed through an acceptable activity, such as sport or competition. These are explanations discussed by researchers, not rules that describe every person.

4

Even among instinct explanations, researchers have disagreed. Some suggest aggression may appear with little or no outside provocation. Others argue that aggression is a response to provocation, such as being insulted or threatened.

5

Social learning theories describe aggression as behavior people can learn. Young people may observe parents, peers or fictional characters and copy what they see. A role model is someone whose behavior is observed and may be imitated. Reinforcement means a response that makes a behavior more likely to happen again. If aggressive behavior brings praise, attention or a reward, or is not challenged, a young person may see it as acceptable. Research has found links between exposure to violent behavior and aggression among children and teenagers; a link does not mean that every viewer will become aggressive. How adults and peers respond to aggression also matters.
